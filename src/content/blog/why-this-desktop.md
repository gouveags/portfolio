Omakub was where I first fell in love with Linux. These days, my own setup is Fedora, Hyprland and Noctalia. I like the way it looks, how quickly I can move around, and how useful the layout is.

Omarchy was part of the inspiration too. I wanted some of that care in my own corner of the internet: a beautiful wallpaper, a quiet interface, and windows that feel good to open and move between.

So I wanted this website to feel a little like my desktop.

## A place that feels like mine

The visual direction was quite specific. True black. Black and white. Elegant, with room for a photograph to do some of the work.

The wallpaper choices come from things I like: Ferrari Formula 1 cars, a classic Porsche 911, Eau Rouge, and São Paulo's Theatro Municipal. They're a way of making the space personal before I've explained very much about myself.

I wanted real photography for that reason. The website is already borrowing the behaviour of a computer; the pictures should give it something familiar and tangible.

When I was describing the idea, I said that entering the page should feel magical. That's a fairly unreasonable requirement for a portfolio, I know. But it gets closer to what I wanted than a list of visual effects.

I wanted the whole thing to feel considered.

<div id="what-i-brought-to-the-web"></div>

## Borrowing the parts I enjoy

On a desktop, I like being able to keep more than one thing open. A page can sit beside another page; I can give one my attention and return to the other later.

That is a useful way to explore someone's work, too. You might want to read about a project and keep an article nearby. Or you might just want to open the career page, read it and leave.

The windows and motion are there to make that movement feel natural. The content still needs to be easy to find and worth reading once you get there.

Underneath the windows, this is a static Astro site. Each page has its own address and can be read without JavaScript, so you can open an article directly and start reading.

There are optional shortcuts if you want them. Turn them on in the navigation guide, then use / to open search and ? to bring the guide back. Arrow keys and Enter work in search, and Escape closes it. You can also use the visible controls throughout.

I enjoy keyboard-driven interfaces. Nobody should need to learn mine before they can read a paragraph.

## The phone gets its own version

I wanted the mobile version to feel closer to an Android home screen: open an app, give it the whole screen, and move between it, Home and Recents.

A phone asks for a different rhythm. Reading should feel comfortable, the controls should be within reach, and moving back should be obvious. The black-and-white direction can stay while the navigation changes.

That's the idea behind this site. A little of the desktop I enjoy using, adapted to a place where I can share my work.

Make yourself at home. The [navigation guide](/guide/) is there if you need it.
